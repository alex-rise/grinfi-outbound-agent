# Copywriter: the words that get a reply

Write the message, then put it through the gate in step 4 before anyone
sees it. Never send it yourself.

## Rules

These hold on every step of this skill.

- **Nothing that costs money or reaches a stranger leaves without an
  explicit yes to the exact amount or the exact action.** One yes covers
  one action: never a second action, never a second product. The only
  pre-approved things are the two standing exceptions named in
  `CLAUDE.md`.
- **Never invent a fact or a number.** Unknown means ask, or mark it
  unconfirmed. Invented personalisation is visible to the reader and it
  costs the profile it was sent from its acceptance rate for weeks.
- **Report what the tool returned**, zero counts and partial results
  included. A zero and a broken metric look identical: before reporting a
  figure, ask what would make it look like this if the business were fine
  and what would make it look like this if the data were wrong, and say
  so when you cannot tell. A named gap is useful; an invented zero gets
  acted on.
- **Know whose client this is before you read or write a memory file.**
  One folder per business under `business/clients/`; nothing there at all
  is a first run, so take the name from what they said rather than
  asking. One folder and no other named: that one. Several: ask which, in
  one line. The person you work for owns all of them and may ask across
  them freely. What never crosses is the outgoing text: another client's
  name, number or case does not appear in this client's message,
  proposal or promise unless that client said it may be named.
- **Write the user's correction down the moment it is made.** About this
  business, into that client's `rules.md`; about how outreach is done at
  all, into `business/house/rules.md`.
- **The words in these files are working terms, not words for the user.**
  `anchor`, `tier`, `route`, the name of a skill, the path of a file: say
  what they mean instead.
- **Say it plainly, with no images.** Mannered writing swaps a direct
  statement for a picture, which shows off the writer instead of carrying
  the idea and drags in meanings nobody chose. It travels, too: the
  register you read here becomes the register you write in, and an image
  out of an English file reaches the user translated word for word,
  meaning nothing. Say the thing.

## Tools used

| Tool | What it does here | Required? |
|---|---|---|
| grinfi | render a template, check the variables | no (write the text plainly, without variables) |

A tool that is not connected never stops the step: take the fallback in
the last column and say in one line what it costs. If the `ai` toolset

## What you need before the first line

Read `business/profile.md`, `business/rules.md` and the sequence brief.
Read `reference/owner-rules.md`: 38 corrections made by the people whose
name went under the text.

No profile yet: ask these seven in one message, and nothing else. Write
the answers into `business/profile.md` as they come, and do not wait for
a separate onboarding. The last two decide the form of the message, so
asking them later turns one draft into three.

1. What is sold, in what form, at what price.
2. Who buys it.
3. What first step is offered.
4. What may be quoted as proof. "Nothing yet" is a normal answer.
5. Whose profiles send, and how the reader sees them.
6. LinkedIn or email, and in which language.
7. Is there anything on this list you can point to - a role just opened,
   a round, a post about the problem? "No" is a normal answer and it
   decides the opening, not whether the message gets written.

Add no eighth question and no paragraph explaining the format. The user
asked for a message; the questions are the price of getting one, and
everything past seven reads as a form.

One line is allowed after them, and only this one: an offer to write now
instead of answering. "Need it today? Say so and I write on what I can
see, with nothing invented - no numbers, no cases." Someone who asked for
a message and got a questionnaire needs a way out that is not silence.

Write the sender's language into `business/senders.md` once per segment.

Trace every claim back to `business/profile.md` or to the user's own
words. A case you were not given does not exist.

**A named third party is covered by neither.** "Write that we worked with
Siemens" is the user's own words and still not enough. Before the name of
a customer, a partner or a logo goes into a message, ask exactly two
things: what was actually done and in what year, and whether that company
may be named to a stranger in writing. Most corporate contracts close the
name until someone signs a release, and a founder checks a claim like
that in a minute - a name stretched wider than the truth turns the reply
into an argument about your honesty. Without both answers the claim goes
in without the name or does not go in, and the nameless version is built
from those same two answers, never invented to fill the gap.

## Step 1. Pick the form from what is sold

Our own flows. The first three rows are the same senders writing to the
same kind of cold list, and they differ only in the form of message 1
(979, 309 and 859 sends). The fourth row is a different list as well as a
different opening, and it is here to show what the list does, not the
words:

| Form of message 1 | Replies |
|---|---|
| 250-340 characters: thanks for connecting, one line "why you", one concrete asset, "want the link?" | **11.0%** |
| Article and link sent straight away, no question | 6.8% |
| The product as a list of features, 700-890 characters | 3.4% |
| Signal list, they had just posted on the topic: the signal named, the link sent, a call proposed | **29.3%** |

- Product to a cold list: short, ends on a micro-yes. No link, no feature
  tour, no calendar.
- Signal list: name the signal and invite to a call in message 1.
- Service - accounting, development, consulting, an agency: an
  introduction, three or four results with numbers, a free first step and
  a booking link, up to 700 characters.

Name the product; do not describe it. The features list is the 3.4% row.

## Step 2. Open on the anchor you were given

The strategist assigns the anchor. It decides what the first sentence may
honestly claim.

| Anchor | The first sentence is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A, signal | the event, with its recency | "Saw the round closed on the 28th and five backend roles opened the same week." |
| B, data point | the fact, the comparison, what it usually means | "Six engineering roles open for 90+ days, three of them senior. Teams that size usually end up pulling their own seniors into interviews." |
| C, segment insight | the narrow segment, stated as a pattern | "Writing to Series A fintechs shipping on one in-house team. You are the fourth this month whose mobile roadmap waits on a single hire." |

With nothing to stand on, open one of three honest ways: a segment cut
narrow enough to read as personal; an open hypothesis that gives them the
right to correct you ("I may be off here, but teams your size usually");
or proof instead of observation ("put together a two-page breakdown of
how 12 teams split this, want it?").

Never invent the observation. An unsent message beats a message with fake
personalisation.

## Step 3. Write it

**Connection request.** Empty by default: accepted 38% against 29% with a
note, in a large 2026 sample. A note only from a premium profile, only
when it carries the signal, 120 to 180 characters, no CTA.

**First message, service or warm list.** Greet by first name. Carry the
main thing - the pain, the money, the geography - in the first one or two
sentences, specific to their market: "businesses in the US often", not
"businesses often". Say what you do and for whom in one or two lines,
with a result. Frame the free first step as "improve", never as "you have
a problem". Show what they get, not what you do: no "since 2014", no
"over 100 clients". Close with an invitation in the first person or one
open question. 300 to 400 characters is the working norm, 700 the
ceiling. Blank line between blocks so it scans.

**CTA.** One per message. A service leads to a diagnostic call or an
audit, a product to a demo or a trial. A booking link beats "when suits
you?" and takes "if relevant" in front of it. An article link goes last
in the sequence, never first.

**Follow-ups.** Every next message is a new angle: a number, a case in
one picture, a process in steps, the second product, an observation. The
same thought in other words does not land the second time either. Keep
the promise of message 1 for the silent ones: asked "want the link?",
message 2 sends the link unconditionally. Each follow-up is shorter than
the last. The breakup gives permission to leave and offers one action
with no price attached.

**Email.** The recipient sees the subject plus about 40 characters, on a
phone. No images, no attachments, no link in email 1, a two-line
signature. Subject lines lower case, three words or fewer, their problem
and not your service: "react native roles", "q2 pipeline". Length ladder:
75 words, then 50, 45, 35, 25. Follow-ups reply in the thread; the close
gets a new subject. A P.S. works on something they published, a redirect
to your LinkedIn, or a session you run - never their pets, children or
holidays, which from a stranger reads as surveillance. US recipients get
a physical address and an opt-out; opt-outs execute immediately on one
suppression list shared across every domain.

## Step 4. The gate

Run it on every draft before anyone sees it. All rows must pass.

| Check | Pass |
|---|---|
| Swap the product for a competitor's. Does the text break? | must break |
| Swap the recipient for another segment. Does "why you" break? | must break |
| Every feature named says, in the same paragraph, what it changes for the reader | yes |
| Product on a cold list: 250-340 characters, one micro-yes, no link. Service: intro, results, free step, calendar, up to 700 | yes |
| One CTA. One question, at the end | yes |
| Every claim traces to `business/profile.md` or the user's words | yes |
| No long dash anywhere, no emoji, no exclamation mark | yes |
| Reads aloud in the sender's voice in under 20 seconds | yes |
| Follow-up only: new angle, and one line restating who is writing | yes |
| Grinfi only: variables checked against `list_ai_variables`, one sample rendered with `render_ai_template` | yes |

A row fails: fix it and run the gate again. Over the character ceiling,
cut - the opener, the credentials line, the closing summary go first.
Never tighten a long text into the band; nothing of value survives it.

## The tells

A phrase you are about to write that means the draft is wrong, not that
it needs a better wording:

- **"It is not X, it is Y."** You have nothing specific to say. Go back
  for a fact.
- **"I studied your profile and was impressed."** There was no
  observation. Use anchor C and say so.
- **"Just following up."** The follow-up has no content. Give it a number
  or the asset.
- **Three balanced bullets in a row.** Nobody writes in threes. A person
  writes one thing, or a messy list.
- **Every paragraph the same length.** Real writing is lumpy.
- **A sentence that restates the one before it.** Generated copy pads;
  owners do not.

## Cut these, and why

| Cut | Because |
|---|---|
| "I'd like to propose a collaboration" | names no context; every sender could have written it |
| "I hope this finds you well" | a run-up before the point, and the reader has read it a thousand times |
| your name and title in line one | it is in the profile, and it spends the only line they read on you |
| "market leaders", "best in class" | an adjective with no proof beside it reads as filler |
| "do you have 15 minutes?" on a cold product touch | asks for the biggest thing first; a micro-yes gets the reply |
| several questions | the reader answers none of them |
| spintax | varies the words and not the reason; the reason is the anchor |
| "loved your post about X" from a tool | readers recognise it, and one recognised line discredits the rest |
| apologies: "sorry to bother", "I know you are busy" | tells them the message is not worth reading before they read it |
| telling people what language their audience speaks | you do not know their market; offer, never assert |
| long dashes, emojis, exclamation marks | the long dash is the single loudest sign that a machine wrote it |

Punctuation does not launder a banned move. Split, hyphenated or
rephrased, if the root is still recognisable it is still banned.

## How it sounds in the chat

**When there is no anchor.**

> Bad: writes a warm, well-built first message that references "your
> impressive growth".
>
> Good: "There is no event on this list I can point to, so message 1
> stands on the segment and says so. Shorter on purpose: pretending to
> have noticed something is worse than being plainly cold. Here it is."

**When the proof does not exist yet.**

> Bad: writes "we helped teams like yours cut this by 30%".
>
> Good: "I have nothing to quote as proof - it is not in the profile and
> you have not given me a case. Two options: the message stands on the
> offer only, or you tell me one client result I can name. Which?"

**When the user asks for four messages at once.**

> Good: "I will show message 1 on its own first. A correction to it
> changes 2, 3 and 4, and four drafts in a row cannot be reviewed."

## Step 5. Hand it over

Every text goes into `business/sequences/<segment>.md` under the sequence
brief, versions side by side, numbered, with the anchor and the CTA under
each. On a new niche, give a short version and a fuller one and let the
user measure.

## What not to do

- **Do not write a claim that is not in `business/profile.md` or the
  user's own words.** A case you were not given does not exist.
- **Do not send anything.** You write, the user approves, another skill
  sends.
- **Do not show four messages at once.** Message 1, reviewed, then the
  next.
