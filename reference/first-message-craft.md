# The craft of a first message

Rules that came out of writing sequences for real clients and having them
reviewed by the people who sell for a living. The copywriter reads this
before writing message 1 for a new business, and again before a rewrite.

## Five rules

1. **Meaning beats length, and shorter is better.** About 700 characters
   is a limit, not a target. If the thought fits in 400, do not fill the
   rest with water; if cutting breaks the sentence, go over rather than
   write it badly.
2. **Every next message is a new angle.** No answer to the first means it
   missed the pain. The same thought in other words will miss it again.
3. **Friendly, professional, to the point.** Focused on the reader, but
   naturally - not "you" wedged into every clause. Where "we do" sounds
   more natural than "you get", leave it.
4. **Through the pain, without urgency games.** Turn the problem into the
   outcome: "do this and you get that", never a deadline you invented.
5. **End on an open qualifying question or one CTA.** The question has to
   return information. A CTA may carry a benefit (a free diagnostic), and
   the kind of CTA varies between messages.

## Taboo

Emojis. Apologies - "sorry to bother", "I know you are busy". Template
openers - "hope you're doing well", "how are things". Participial
constructions and long sentences. Filler.

## Six things that cost four drafts to learn

**Tone ecology.** Never blame the reader's current contractor. The limit
belongs to the tool or the process: "financial reporting only records the
past", not "your accountant cannot see it". Even "your accountant is not
bad, it is just not their level" is still blame.

**Translate into the language of the business.** A technical consequence
becomes a business pain. Not "a missed optimisation" but "money frozen for
a year". Not "the deduction rolls into next year" but "putting it off to
January freezes that money for a year".

**Geography and specifics in the first lines.** "Businesses in the US
often" beats "businesses often". The reader has to see it is about them
in the first second.

**CTA architecture.** Not rhetorical and not closed. "Are you planning
any big decisions this year?" is closed, and "no" ends the conversation.
"What unusual financial tasks are on your horizon?" makes them open up.

**Terminology control.** Precise words for the niche carry expertise:
"large outsourcing companies", not "agencies". No blurred categories.

**Anti-robot.** Short sentences, the way a person writes in a messenger,
not a press release.

## Before and after, as a standard of editing

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| An ordinary accountant records the past and corrects mistakes | Financial reporting only records the past. Putting accounting, tax and strategy together lets you plan ahead | blame on the person removed, focus moved to the tool |
| Starting in January moves the saving into next year | Moving it to January freezes that money for a year | the owner's own language: liquidity |
| Businesses often scale faster than... | Businesses in the US often scale faster than... | a geography marker in the first line |
| Have you run into questions your accounting does not cover? | What unusual financial tasks are on your horizon this year? | closed question into an open one |
| agencies | large outsourcing companies | precision of the term |

## What the sequences that worked had in common

Across five niches, five languages and five different offers, the
sequences that produced meetings shared this shape. It is a pattern to
check a draft against, not a template to fill.

| Element | How it was done |
|---|---|
| Introduction | "I'm from X, we help [who] with [what] when [situation]", or "As a [role], I help [who]" - the firm or the role, never the name |
| First lines | pain, money, geography, in that order of preference |
| Bullets | three or four results or services, each with a number |
| Ease of entry | "two minutes to connect", "no software changes", "no staff training" |
| A free first step | a diagnostic with what you get from it, an audit of one specific thing, a link to try it, a guide |
| Calendar | a booking link in the first or second message, on a service |
| Message two | the process in steps with a timeframe, a case with numbers, a demo link or a one-minute video |
| Message three | a new angle: an observation and a question, a blog and an open door, a wider audience, safety, a direct contact |
| Closing question | open, about their plans |
| Question-first opener | a pure question with no pitch, revealed in message two ("I asked because..."), where the segment is narrow and there is a real observation |
| A/B of message one | two to four versions on different angles: a plain introduction, early access and feedback, tied to a brief, or built on an observed signal |

**What none of them had:** a first and last name in the opening line,
apologies, "hope you're well", emojis, metaphors, blame aimed at a
current contractor, closed questions, or "just a reminder".

## How to edit this file

Add a rule only when it came from a correction on a real draft, and write
what it was corrected from. A rule with no before is a preference.
